The short version

  • Road signs and the law come first. A posted clearance, weight limit, truck restriction or closure always wins over what the app says.
  • The app can be wrong. Bridge heights, truck restrictions, parking, prices, weather and hours of service come from outside sources that can be missing, late or incorrect.
  • You drive the truck. You decide where to go and whether a route, a stop or a parking spot is safe and legal for your vehicle.

Who can use TruckBuddy

TruckBuddy is for professional drivers and people who work with them in the United States. You must be at least 18 years old.

Using the app while driving

  • Mount your phone and set it up before you drive. Talk to the copilot by voice; a single tap starts it.
  • Do not hold the phone, type or read long text while the vehicle is moving. Follow FMCSA rules and the laws of every state you drive in on the use of mobile phones.
  • Pull over to change settings, your truck profile or anything that takes more than one touch.

Routes, bridges and restrictions

  • Routes are planned for the truck height, length, weight and hazmat status in your truck profile. Keep it correct; a wrong height means a wrong route.
  • Clearance and restriction data come from HERE, public bridge inventories and OpenStreetMap. Not every bridge, tunnel, overhang or local restriction is in them. The app warns you about what it knows; it cannot warn you about what it does not know.
  • If the app says it could not find a legal truck route, do not drive it without checking the restrictions yourself or getting a permit.

Parking, places, prices and hours of service

  • Satellite parking checks, entrance directions and truck parking counts are estimates. A lot may be full, closed, private or not allowed for trucks.
  • Place details, phone numbers and fuel prices may be out of date. What drivers say about a place comes from Google Maps and reflects other people’s opinions.
  • Hours-of-service clocks come from your ELD provider. Your logs and your ELD, not TruckBuddy, are the record that counts.
  • In a breakdown or emergency, call 911 or your carrier. TruckBuddy does not call for help.

TruckBuddy is free while in early access. To keep it running, there is a daily limit on copilot questions and satellite checks; the map and routes keep working past it. Features may change, and the service may be unavailable at times.
